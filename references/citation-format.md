# ACS Citation Format — Author-Number (Numbered) System

**Source of truth for this file:** empirically derived from the in-text
citations and full reference lists of two published JACS articles supplied
as tone references (`J. Am. Chem. Soc. 2021, 143, 12715–12724` and
`J. Am. Chem. Soc. 2024, 146, 3125–3135`), cross-checked against the
well-established ACS "numbered" citation system. The official ACS Style
Quick Guide page (`pubs.acs.org/books/book/4293/chapter/244189111/`) could
not be fetched in this environment (network policy blocks `pubs.acs.org`);
if the user pastes that page's text, fold it in and remove this note.

This is the system the user specified: **numbered, superscript in-text
citations; references listed in citation order** (not alphabetically, not
author-date).

## In-text citation placement

Observed pattern across both source papers:

- Citation numbers are **superscript**, with **no space** before them and
  **no brackets or parentheses**.
- A number attaches to the specific word, phrase, or clause it supports —
  **not** necessarily to the end of the sentence. If a citation supports a
  clause in the middle of a sentence, the number goes right after that
  clause, before the sentence continues.
- When the citation ends a sentence, the number follows the closing
  punctuation (period, comma) with no space:
  > "...plays a crucial role in its function and dysfunction.¹,²"
  > "...sequence-based design,⁴,⁵ structure-based design,⁶⁻¹¹ and
  > high-throughput screening (HTS),¹²⁻¹⁷ have been employed..."
- **Multiple citations** at one point: numbers separated by commas, no
  spaces: `4,5`
- **Consecutive runs of three or more**: collapsed to a range with an en
  dash: `6–11` (not `6,7,8,9,10,11`)
- A single sentence can carry several citation points, each attached to the
  specific claim it supports — don't pile every citation for a paragraph
  onto the final period.

## Reference list

- References are numbered **in the order they are first cited** in the
  text (not alphabetical), and that number is permanent for that source
  throughout the paper.
- Entry format for a **journal article**:

  ```
  (N)  Last, F. M.; Last, F. M.; Last, F. M. Title of the Article in
       Sentence Case. Abbreviated Journal Name Year, Volume, StartPage–EndPage.
  ```

  Real examples from the source papers:
  > (1) Biswal, H. S.; Wategaonkar, S. Nature of the N-H···S Hydrogen
  > Bond. *J. Phys. Chem. A* **2009**, *113*, 12763−12773.

  > (2) Chand, A.; Sahoo, D. K.; Rana, A.; Jena, S.; Biswal, H. S. The
  > Prodigious Hydrogen Bonds with Sulfur and Selenium in Molecular
  > Assemblies, Structural Biology, and Functional Materials. *Acc. Chem.
  > Res.* **2020**, *53*, 1580−1592.

  Notes on the formatting conventions visible in these entries:
  - Authors: `Last, F. M.;` for each author, semicolon-separated, **no
    "and"** before the last author, no comma between last name and
    initials beyond the one shown.
  - Article title: sentence case (only first word, proper nouns, and
    established chemical/formula capitalization capitalized), ends with a
    period, **not italicized**.
  - Journal name: standard ACS/CAS abbreviation (e.g., *J. Phys. Chem. A*,
    *Acc. Chem. Res.*, *J. Am. Chem. Soc.*), *italicized*.
  - Year: **bold**, followed by a comma.
  - Volume: *italicized*, followed by a comma.
  - Pages: start–end page numbers joined with an en dash, ending with a
    period.

- Entry format for a **book / software with editors or a publisher**
  (example from the source paper's reference list, a Gaussian citation):

  > (75) Frisch, M. J.; Trucks, G. W.; Schlegel, H. B.; Scuseria, G. E.;
  > Robb, M. A.; Cheeseman, J. R.; Scalmani, G.; Barone, V.; Petersson, G.
  > A.; Nakatsuji, H. et al. *Gaussian 16*, Revision A.03; Gaussian Inc.:
  > Wallingford, CT, 2016.

  Pattern: author list (`et al.` allowed for long author lists), *italicized
  title*, edition/revision, publisher: city, year — semicolon-separated
  fields, period at the end.
